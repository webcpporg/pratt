[#pratt-errors]
== Errors

pratt reports every failure as a value, and throws nothing of its own. Every
operation that can fail, every nud and every led returns a
`pratt::result<T>`, which is Boost.System's `result`: a `T`, or the
`boost::system::error_code` of the failure. pratt's own errors are the
enumerators of `pratt::error`, in a category of their own,
`pratt::error_category()`, whose name is `webcpp.pratt`. An `error` converts
to an `error_code` and compares with one, so a program checks a failure as
`parsed.error() == pratt::error::trailing_input`, and writes it with
`parsed.error().message()`.

[cols="3,1,3,5",options="header"]
|===
| Enumerator | Value | Message | When

| `unexpected_token`
| 1
| unexpected token
| A token stands where the grammar has no nud or led for it: `)` where an
  operand is due, `= 3` in the micro-subset.

| `unexpected_end`
| 2
| unexpected end of input
| The input ends where a token was still due: `1 +`; an unclosed string
  of the micro-subset.

| `expected_closing_token`
| 3
| expected closing token
| A grouping, or a call of the micro-subset, misses its `)`: `(1 + 2`.

| `trailing_input`
| 4
| trailing input after expression
| Tokens remain after a whole expression: `1 2`. `parse` returns it.

| `invalid_assignment_target`
| 5
| invalid assignment target
| `=`, `{plus}{plus}` or `--` of the calculator on what is not a variable:
  `5 = 3`, `3{plus}{plus}`, `(1) = 2`.

| `undefined_variable`
| 6
| undefined variable
| The calculator reads a variable that the environment does not hold.

| `invalid_character`
| 7
| invalid character
| A character that starts no token: `1 $ 2`, or `!` without `=` in the
  calculator.

| `nesting_too_deep`
| 8
| expression nested too deeply
| A parse would nest deeper than its `max_depth` (<<pratt-limits>>).

| `division_by_zero`
| 9
| division by zero
| The calculator's `/` or `%` by zero, or `0 ^ -1`.
|===

The values are fixed: a value is never reused for another meaning, so a
value stored or sent keeps its meaning from one version of pratt to the
next, and 0 is no error.

The engine and its helpers produce `nesting_too_deep`, `trailing_input` and
`expected_closing_token` themselves; every other error is a grammar's
choice, and the enumerators are there for a grammar to use, as the
calculator and the micro-subset do. A grammar, or an environment of the
calculator, may return an error of its own category instead, and pratt
passes it on unchanged, as <<pratt-calculator-environment>> shows.

A grammar returns an error into its `result` as `return
pratt::error::unexpected_token;`. When its node type converts from an
`error` too, as a Boost.Multiprecision number does, `result` refuses that
conversion as ambiguous, and the grammar builds the error in place, as the
engine and the calculator always do:
`return {boost::system::in_place_error, pratt::error::unexpected_token};`.
